\begin{abstract}
This thesis studies an alternative way of training a compact neural network for tabular machine-learning problems. Instead of using backpropagation to optimise all weights jointly, the proposed prototype trains the hidden representation of a single-hidden-layer Multilayer Perceptron with an Evolution Strategy. The key idea is a Michigan-style representation: each individual in the evolutionary population represents one hidden neuron, not a complete neural network. Parent neurons and mutated offspring neurons form a candidate pool, a credit-assignment mechanism selects the surviving neurons, and the selected population becomes the hidden layer.

Once the hidden activations are fixed, the output layer is fitted separately. The final evaluation keeps the output model aligned with the task: binary classification uses an unregularised logistic output trained with cross-entropy, regression uses Ridge regression with Generalised Cross-Validation, and multiclass classification uses a softmax output. This separation is important because Ridge/GCV gives an analytical solve for regression, while logistic and softmax outputs provide probabilistic predictions for classification.

The experimental evaluation compares neuron-selection strategies, standard tabular baselines, logistic and Ridge ablations, regression variants, sparse logistic output filtering, multiclass softmax models, and a controlled Michigan-versus-Pittsburgh evolutionary benchmark. The results do not show universal superiority over all classical models, but they support the main hypothesis: under the tested settings, evolving individual hidden neurons can produce a useful hidden representation and can offer a favourable accuracy-time trade-off compared with evolving complete hidden layers.
\end{abstract}

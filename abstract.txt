This thesis studies an alternative way of training a compact neural network for tabular machine-learning problems. Instead of using backpropagation to optimise all weights jointly, the proposed prototype trains the hidden representation of a single-hidden-layer Multilayer Perceptron with an Evolution Strategy. The key idea is a Michigan-style representation: each individual in the evolutionary population represents one hidden neuron, not a complete neural network. Parent neurons and mutated offspring neurons form a candidate pool, a credit-assignment mechanism selects the surviving neurons, and the selected population becomes the hidden layer.

Once the hidden activations are fixed, the output layer is fitted separately. The main architecture uses Ridge regression with Generalised Cross-Validation, which provides an analytical output-layer solve and an automatic choice of the Ridge regularisation parameter. The project also studies related extensions, including regression, Lasso-based output sparsification, and an unregularised logistic/softmax output layer for probabilistic classification.

The experimental evaluation compares neuron-selection strategies, standard tabular baselines, Ridge ablations, regression variants, sparse output filtering, multiclass softmax models, and a controlled Michigan-versus-Pittsburgh evolutionary benchmark. The results do not show universal superiority over all classical models, but they support the main hypothesis: under the tested settings, evolving individual hidden neurons can produce a useful hidden representation and can offer a favourable accuracy-time trade-off compared with evolving complete hidden layers.
